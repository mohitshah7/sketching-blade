The story is about a seventeen-year-old girl named Ayame, born into the comfort of an upper-middle-class family—a life that, to most, would seem enviable. Yet to her, it felt suffocating. Each day blurred into the next, a silent monotony she longed to escape.

It was a Saturday, the kind of day that drifted by without consequence. Ayame was walking home from school, her thoughts a quiet storm, when the peace of the street was broken by a sudden clatter.

A cabbage cart rolled past her in a chaotic rush, the seller chasing after it, shouting in despair, “Ahh! My cabbages!” The cart barreled down the road before slamming into the side of a parked car with a metallic thud.

The commotion drew a crowd. The car’s owner stormed toward the scene, his voice sharp and accusatory. “Who? Who was responsible for this? I need answers!”

The cabbage seller, eyes darting nervously, pointed at Ayame. “Sir! She’s the one. She pushed the cart down the street—a delinquent’s move!”

Ayame froze, her chest tightening. “Wait, what? Why are you blaming me for your mistake? Sir, please, believe me—this man is lying. He pushed his own cart!” Her voice wavered, caught between fear and disbelief.

The car owner scoffed, his tone dripping with condescension. “Why would he push his own cart? Do you take me for a fool? The cart scratched my car, and you’re going to pay for it.”

The crowd murmured in judgment, and Ayame’s eyes stung. She felt the tears building—until a warm, steady hand gripped hers.

“Come on,” a voice said quietly but firmly.

Before she could react, she was being pulled through the crowd. Someone shouted behind them, “Hey! Delinquent girl, where do you think you’re going? Someone stop her!” But by the time anyone thought to follow, they were gone.

They didn’t stop running until they reached a quieter street. Ayame bent over, gasping for breath, but her rescuer seemed unfazed, his gaze fixed ahead—on a small dog trotting along the sidewalk.

‘Why isn’t he tired after running so fast? And… who is he? Should I ask? Wait—half of his hair is red. What a strange style,’ Ayame thought, her curiosity momentarily distracting her from the panic.

“Hey,” she finally said, her voice softer now, “it was really kind of you to help me… but who are you? And why did you help me?”

The boy glanced at her with a calm, reassuring smile. “I just like to help people… And besides, we’re classmates—or we used to be. We were in the same class in middle school. I figured I’d help you as a friend.”

Ayame blinked, searching her memory. ‘He’s… handsome. Calm. And his skin is so pale. How could I not remember someone like him?’

“I’m sorry,” she admitted, “but I don’t remember you.”

He lowered his gaze, the faintest smile touching his lips. “I figured as much.”

The guilt in her chest deepened. “Are you going the same way? If so, let me treat you to something as thanks.”

“It’s okay,” he replied, his tone as steady as before. “I don’t need anything to eat. Just walking with you is enough for me.”

So they walked, side by side, in a quiet rhythm. And though the street was calm again, Ayame couldn’t shake the strange warmth that had begun to settle in her heart.
