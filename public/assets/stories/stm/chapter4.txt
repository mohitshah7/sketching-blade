Ayame’s eyes froze on the photograph. The frame was decorated with white lilies, a single incense stick burnt out beneath it. It was the kind of arrangement she had only ever seen in memory of the dead. The boy in the picture, his familiar black hair and sharp yet gentle eyes, was unmistakable.

It was Akio.

Her voice trembled, almost breaking as she turned to the old woman.
“W, why is this photo, like this?”

The granny’s expression softened, though it carried the weight of sorrow etched deep into her wrinkles. She lowered her shopping basket to the floor and spoke slowly, as though every word might wound Ayame further.
“Because, child… Akio is no longer with us. It has been a year since we lost him.”

Ayame staggered back a step, her breath catching in her throat.
“No, no, that can’t be true! You’re mistaken!” she protested, shaking her head violently, strands of her hair brushing across her tearful eyes. “I saw him, I met him just two days ago. He spoke to me, he walked with me… he helped me…”

The old woman let out a faint laugh, though a tear slid down her cheek as she looked at the photograph.
“Yes… that sounds just like him,” her voice cracked, “always trying to help, even now.”

Ayame’s heart pounded, confusion swarming her like a thousand whispers.
“But how…?” she whispered, her hands trembling.

The granny lowered herself onto a wooden stool and sighed deeply, staring at the floor as if replaying a memory she could not erase.
“It happened at the river bridge outside town. They said a car, it hit him while he was trying to save a stray dog. It was quick, they told me. He didn’t suffer much.”

The words cut into Ayame’s chest like blades. Her knees weakened, and she clutched the edge of the doorframe for support.
“The bridge…” she whispered, her eyes widening, “the same bridge he mentioned, he told me that’s where I should stop lingering in the past…”

Her thoughts spiraled, tangled between reality and impossibility. How could she have spoken to someone who had been gone for a year? His voice still echoed in her ears, his presence still warm in her memory.

The old woman’s gaze returned to her, sharp yet tender.
“What is it, child? You seem shaken.”

Ayame’s lips quivered as she forced the words out.
“This can’t be true. It’s… impossible. I met him. He was there. He was real…”

The granny closed her eyes, a fragile smile forming on her lips.
“Perhaps you did, Ayame,” a tear fell again, but her voice steadied, “perhaps Akio found a way, to guide you one last time.”

Ayame’s chest tightened. She wanted to deny it, to scream that it wasn’t possible, but the truth stared back at her from that framed photograph, unyielding.

She lowered her eyes, her heart racing between grief, confusion, and a strange sense of awe. Was it his spirit that had been beside her all along? If so, why her?

And in the heavy silence of that old wooden house, Ayame felt both more lost, and more guided, than she ever had before.
